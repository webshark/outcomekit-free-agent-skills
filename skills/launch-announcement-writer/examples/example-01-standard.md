# Example: Offline mode for a field-service app

## Scenario
A founder shipped offline mode in the mobile app at 11am and wants the announcement out the same afternoon.

## Input
- **What shipped**: "Offline mode in the mobile app. Cleaners can see their route, mark jobs complete, and add photos with no signal. It syncs when they are back on data. Before this, if they lost signal in a basement or on a rural job, the app was useless and they had to phone the office."
- **Audience and CTA**: "Owners of cleaning companies, 5 to 50 staff. Most found us through Facebook groups. I want them to update the app."
- **Voice reference**: "We post plainly. Our best post last year was just 'we fixed the thing you kept emailing us about'."

## Expected output
**Worth a standalone announcement.** This is a capability change, not a fix — the app now works somewhere it previously did not, which is the kind of thing this audience tells each other about.

**X post** (48 words)

Your cleaners can now use the app with no signal.

Routes, job completion, photos — all work in a basement or on a rural job, and everything syncs when they are back on data.

No more phoning the office because the app went blank.

Update the app: [link]

**LinkedIn post** (94 words)

Cleaners lose signal in basements and on rural jobs, and until now the app went blank when they did.

As of this morning the app works with no signal. Your team can see their route, mark jobs complete, and add photos. It all syncs when they are back on data.

It is not a new product. It is the difference between a cleaner finishing the job and a cleaner stuck phoning the office.

If your team works basements, rural properties, or anywhere the signal drops, this one is for you.

Update the app: [link]

**Email**

- Subject A: "The app now works with no signal"
- Subject B: "We fixed the basement problem"
- Body:

  Hi [first name],

  A cleaner in a basement, no signal, app blank, phone call to the office. That should not happen any more.

  As of today the app works with no signal. Your cleaners can see their route, mark jobs complete, and add photos, and it all syncs when they are back on data.

  Update the app: [link]

  [screenshot]

**Call to action across all three: Update the app.** Same words everywhere, so someone who sees two of the three reads it as one message rather than two campaigns.

**Voice notes**: The reference post is plain and slightly self-deprecating, so none of these open with "excited to announce" and none use the phrase "offline mode" before describing what it does. "The basement problem" in subject B comes from the founder's own example of where the app failed.

## Why this is a good result
All three versions lead with what changed for the cleaner rather than with the feature name, and each is written for its format instead of being the same paragraph three times — the X post is 48 words, the LinkedIn post earns its length with the line about what the change actually is, and the email opens on a scene rather than an announcement. One call to action is repeated verbatim across all three, and the voice reference is honored specifically: plain language, no launch-day hype, and the basement and rural-job scenes taken from the founder's own description.
