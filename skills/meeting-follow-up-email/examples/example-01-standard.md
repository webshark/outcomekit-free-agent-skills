# Example: Recap after a 30-minute prospect call

## Scenario
A founder comes off a half-hour call with an operations director and wants the recap out before the next meeting starts.

## Input
- **Call notes**: "Call w/ Rob Feeney, ops director at Castleford Bakery, 4 sites. Want to fix ordering across sites — each site manager orders separately, no visibility. Rob wants one dashboard. Said budget 'probably somewhere around 8-10k' but then said finance signs off, not him. Talked about starting with 2 sites as a pilot, he liked that. Asked about integration with their supplier portal, I said I would check. Wants it before busy season, which is late Sept. His colleague Aisha was on the call, barely spoke, I think she runs finance ops."
- **Recipient and goal**: "Rob. I want him to confirm the two-site pilot and tell me who signs off on budget."

## Expected output
Subject: Recap — two-site pilot and the supplier portal question

Hi Rob, thanks for the half hour. Quick recap so we are working from the same notes.

**What we agreed**
- The problem to fix: each site orders separately today and nobody has a shared view
- You want one dashboard across the sites

**Next steps** (dates are my proposals)
- Me: confirm whether we can integrate with your supplier portal — back to you by Thursday 30 July
- Rob: confirm the two-site pilot and which two sites you want — by Friday 31 July

**Still open**
- Who signs off on budget? You mentioned finance — is that Aisha, or someone else?
- Is late September a hard deadline or the preference?

If I have any of this wrong, just reply on that line.

---

**Three things handled differently, and why**
- **The pilot.** Rob liked the idea but has not agreed to it, and confirming it is the goal of the email, so it is a next step for him rather than something listed as agreed.
- **The 8-10k figure.** Rob said "probably" and then said he does not sign off. Putting a number in a recap turns a guess into an anchor you will negotiate down from. It belongs in the scope document, after the approver is known.
- **"Aisha runs finance ops I think."** The uncertainty is the point, so it became the first open question rather than an assumption stated as fact in a written record.

## Why this is a good result
The recap runs to about 125 words and every next step carries an owner and a specific date, including the ones the sender owes. The two things the operator was genuinely unsure about — the budget figure and Aisha's role — became an omission and a question rather than statements that would be awkward to walk back later. Ending with a one-line correction invitation is what produces a reply, and that reply is the written record of what was agreed.
